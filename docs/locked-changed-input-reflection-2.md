The results of the test matched my expectations. Moving from 85% SGOV / 15% VOO to 10% SGOV / 90% VOO increased the estimated return of the portfolio from 5.28% to 13.13% but increased the volatility of the portfolio from 2.45% to 14.11%. The increase in risk was three times greater than the increase in return and the return per unit of risk decreased significantly. This test cannot prove my “especially in down markets” claim because volatility is the measure of the swings of a portfolio in both directions. A portfolio of 90% stocks could fall 25-35% in a down year, which is not captured by the 14.11% volatility of the portfolio. The PASS result for the test only means that the portfolio met the return and reserve requirements for the test. It does not mean that the portfolio is suitable for my goal of capital preservation. Therefore, my recommendation remains the same as before. I will retain the original 85% SGOV / 15% VOO portfolio because the additional return does not justify the additional volatility and downside risk for a portfolio with a capital preservation goal.
